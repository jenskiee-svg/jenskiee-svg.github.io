My Story

My name is Jenelyn Auditor, and I am a Bachelor of Science in Criminology student at the University of the Cordilleras. I was born in January 2006 and grew up in Rizal Province. I am the youngest among five siblings, with two brothers and three sisters. Today, I live alone in Baguio City to pursue my studies while continuing my journey as a student-athlete. Leaving my family was one of the biggest challenges I have ever faced, but it also became one of the greatest lessons of my life. Living independently has taught me responsibility, resilience, and the importance of believing in myself even when things become difficult.

When I was a child, school was my favorite place. Every morning, I looked forward to attending classes because learning was fun and I loved spending time with my classmates. Life was simple back then. During recess and after class, we would play Chinese garter, text, pogs, holen, and goma. Those moments became some of the happiest memories of my childhood. Mathematics was always my favorite subject because I enjoyed solving problems and challenging myself to find the correct answers. Because of my love for math, my dream when I was young was to become a Mathematics teacher. I also loved dancing and happily joined school performances whenever I had the chance.

I completed my elementary education at Mabuhay Homes 2000 Elementary School, where I not only built wonderful memories but also developed the value of hard work. Through dedication and perseverance, I graduated with Honors and received the Best in Mathematics and Best in MAPEH awards. These recognitions made me realize that when you work hard and stay committed, your efforts will always be rewarded. My elementary years became the foundation of my confidence, discipline, and love for learning.

I continued my studies at Vicente Madrigal Integrated School for junior high school. High school was not exactly how I imagined it would be because I spent much of my time representing our school in sports competitions. Because of this, I often missed classes and had fewer opportunities to spend time with my classmates. Then the COVID-19 pandemic changed everything. Face-to-face classes suddenly stopped, and learning shifted to online and modular classes. It was a difficult adjustment because I missed being inside the classroom, interacting with my teachers, and making memories with my friends. Although I was physically distant from many people, those years taught me how to become more adaptable, patient, and independent. Despite the challenges, I continued to work hard in my studies and graduated with Honors, proving to myself that it is possible to balance academics and sports with determination and discipline.

For senior high school, I studied at STI College Ortigas-Cainta under the Humanities and Social Sciences (HUMSS) strand. This became one of the most memorable chapters of my life because I met people with different personalities, perspectives, and dreams. I built meaningful friendships, became more confident, and learned lessons that helped me mature as a person. It was also during this time that I realized my childhood dream had changed. As I grew older, I wanted a career where I could directly help and protect people, making a positive impact on the lives of others.

That realization led me to study Criminology at the University of the Cordilleras. College has been the most challenging stage of my life so far. It is stressful, exhausting, and sometimes overwhelming, but it is also filled with unforgettable memories and life-changing experiences. For the first time, I lived far away from my family. At first, I struggled with homesickness and the responsibilities of living alone. I learned how to cook, although I still watch TikTok tutorials from time to time. I learned how to wash my clothes, clean my room, manage my allowance, and become responsible for myself. Living independently taught me that growing up means learning to solve problems on your own while continuing to move forward.

Living in Baguio City also allowed me to meet people from different provinces, cultures, traditions, and languages. Every person I met taught me something new, and every experience broadened my understanding of the world. College introduced me to more difficult subjects and showed me the realities of life. It was during this time that I realized my true dream—to become a member of the Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP). I admire firefighters because they willingly risk their own lives to save others. Their courage, discipline, and selfless service inspire me to become someone who can protect lives, respond during emergencies, and serve the community with integrity and compassion.

Sports have always been one of the biggest parts of my life. My journey as an athlete started in Grade 5, when a friend introduced me to athletics. From that day on, I fell in love with running and continued joining competitions. However, in Grade 7, I suffered an injury that forced me to stop competing. It was heartbreaking because I had to leave behind something I truly loved. After five long years, I finally returned to the track. Everything felt different. I became tired more quickly, and my speed was no longer the same. My first competition after returning was here in Baguio City. I was nervous and unsure if I could still compete, but I gave everything I had and finished in third place. For many people, it may have been just a bronze medal, but for me, it symbolized courage, determination, and a fresh start. During the most recent BBEAL, I earned second place, proving to myself that improvement comes through patience, consistency, and hard work. My journey as an athlete has taught me that setbacks are never the end of the story. Sometimes, they become the reason we come back even stronger.

Outside academics and sports, I enjoy expressing myself through drawing, dancing, running, creating videos, taking photographs, traveling, and exploring nature. These hobbies allow me to relax, appreciate life's simple moments, and maintain a healthy balance between my responsibilities and personal happiness.

Looking back on my journey, I realize that every experience—whether joyful or painful—has helped shape the person I am today. From a little girl who loved going to school and dreamed of becoming a Mathematics teacher, I have grown into a young woman pursuing Criminology while living independently in Baguio City and proudly representing the University of the Cordilleras as a student-athlete. Along the way, I discovered new dreams, faced unexpected challenges, and learned lessons that no classroom alone could teach.

Through every challenge, I learned that life will not always go according to plan. Dreams may change, setbacks may happen, and the road may become difficult, but perseverance, faith, and hard work will always help me move forward. My injury taught me patience, living away from my family taught me independence, and every success and failure taught me humility and resilience. These experiences have made me stronger, wiser, and more determined to pursue my goals.

As I continue writing the next chapters of my life, I carry with me the values of discipline, integrity, compassion, and perseverance. My dream of becoming a member of the Bureau of Fire Protection is more than a career goal—it is my way of giving back to the community by protecting lives and serving others with courage and dedication. I know that many challenges still lie ahead, but I face them with confidence, hope, and faith in God. This is only the beginning of my story, and I will continue striving to become not only a successful professional but also a person who inspires others through hard work, resilience, and a genuine heart for service.
